Phana is a photon analogue that carry energy. They are short-lived and decay after minutes, transferring their energy into the surrounding environment. Phana particles possess wavelength and frequency similar to light, determined at emission and through interaction with matter.  
  
Their electromagnetic properties can also be altered by manon, though doing so requires adjusting the energy contained within the phana.  
  
During emission, the wavelength of phana depends on the energy of the emission, forming a spectrum from infrared (low energy), through visible light, to ultraviolet (high energy). The brightness of phana is determined by the quantity produced, with greater emission resulting in higher luminosity regardless of wavelength.  
  
Phana are naturally emitted in a diffuse and incoherent state, radiating outward in all directions. Through the application of manon, this emission can be aligned and focused, increasing coherence and allowing phana to form directed beams or structured effects.  
  
When phana interacts with [raw mana](0101-mana.md), the raw mana will convert itself into additional phana alongside its electromagnetic properties during interaction, resulting in a cascading effect.  
  
Uses of phana:  
- Light source  
- Telecommunications  
- Wireless networks  
- A mana-taxing weapon (ionizing radiation)
